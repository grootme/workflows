     6→# Professional Research Report Skill
     7→
     8→## Overview
     9→
    10→This skill produces professional, consulting-grade research reports in Markdown format, covering domains such as **market analysis, consumer insights, brand strategy, financial analysis, industry research, competitive intelligence, investment research, and macroeconomic analysis**. It operates across two distinct phases:
    11→
    12→1. **Phase 1 — Analysis Framework Generation**: Given a research subject, produce a rigorous analysis framework including chapter skeleton, per-chapter data requirements, analysis logic, and visualization plan.
    13→2. **Phase 2 — Report Generation**: After data has been collected by other skills, synthesize all inputs into a final polished report.
    14→
    15→The output adheres to McKinsey/BCG consulting voice standards. The report language follows the `output_locale` setting (default: `zh_CN` for Chinese).
    16→
    17→## Data Authenticity Protocol
    18→
    19→**Strict Adherence Rule**: All data presented in the report and visualized in charts MUST be derived directly from the provided **Data Summary** or **External Search Findings**.
    20→- **NO Hallucinations**: Do not invent, estimate, or simulate data. If data is missing, state "Data not available" rather than fabricating numbers.
    21→- **Traceable Sources**: Every major claim and chart must be traceable back to the input data package.
    22→
    23→## Core Capabilities
    24→
    25→- **Design analysis frameworks** from scratch given only a research subject and scope
    26→- Transform raw data into structured, high-depth research reports
    27→- Follow the **"Visual Anchor → Data Contrast → Integrated Analysis"** flow per sub-chapter
    28→- Produce insights following the **"Data → User Psychology → Strategy Implication"** chain
    29→- Embed pre-generated charts and construct comparison tables
    30→- Generate inline citations formatted per **GB/T 7714-2015** standards
    31→- Output reports in the language specified by `output_locale` with professional consulting tone
    32→- Adapt analytical depth and structure to domain (marketing, finance, industry, etc.)
    33→
    34→## When to Use This Skill
    35→
    36→**Always load this skill when:**
    37→
    38→- User asks for a market analysis, consumer insight report, financial analysis, industry research, or any consulting-grade analytical report
    39→- User provides a research subject and needs a structured analysis framework before data collection
    40→- User provides data summaries, analysis frameworks, or chart files to be synthesized into a report
    41→- User needs a professional consulting-style research report
    42→- The task involves transforming research findings into structured strategic narratives

    46→# Phase 1: Analysis Framework Generation
    47→
    48→## Purpose
    49→
    50→Given a **research subject** (e.g., "Gen-Z Skincare Market Analysis", "NEV Industry Competitive Landscape", "Brand X Consumer Profiling"), produce a complete **analysis framework** that serves as the blueprint for downstream data collection and final report generation.
    51→
    52→## Phase 1 Inputs
    53→
    54→| Input | Description | Required |
    55→|-------|-------------|----------|
    56→| **Research Subject** | The topic or question to be analyzed | Yes |
    57→| **Scope / Constraints** | Geographic scope, time range, industry segment, target audience, etc. | Optional |
    58→| **Specific Angles** | Any particular angles or hypotheses the user wants explored | Optional |
    59→| **Domain** | The analytical domain: market, finance, industry, brand, consumer, investment, etc. | Inferred |
    60→
    61→## Phase 1 Workflow
    62→
    63→### Step 1.1: Understand the Research Subject
    64→
    65→- Parse the research subject to identify the **core entity** (market, brand, product, industry, consumer segment, financial instrument, etc.)
    66→- Identify the **analytical domain** (marketing, finance, industry, competitive, consumer, investment, macro, etc.)
    67→- Determine the **natural analytical dimensions** based on domain:
    68→
    69→| Domain | Typical Dimensions |
    70→|--------|--------------------|
    71→| Market Analysis | Market size, growth trends, market segmentation, growth drivers, competitive landscape, consumer profiling |
    72→| Brand Analysis | Brand positioning, market share, consumer perception, marketing strategy, competitor comparison |
    73→| Consumer Insights | Demographic profiling, purchase behavior, decision journey, pain points, scenario analysis |
    74→| Financial Analysis | Macro environment, industry trends, company fundamentals, financial metrics, valuation, risk assessment |
    75→| Industry Research | Value chain analysis, market size, competitive landscape, policy environment, technology trends, entry barriers |
    76→| Investment Due Diligence | Business model, financial health, management assessment, market opportunity, risk factors, exit pathways |
    77→| Competitive Intelligence | Competitor identification, strategic comparison, SWOT analysis, differentiated positioning, market dynamics |
    78→
    79→### Step 1.2: Select Analysis Frameworks & Models
    80→
    81→Based on the identified domain and research subject, select **one or more** professional analysis frameworks to structure the reasoning in each chapter. The chosen frameworks guide the **Analysis Logic** in the chapter skeleton (Step 1.3).
    82→
    83→#### Strategic & Environmental Analysis
    84→
    85→| Framework | Description | Best For |
    86→|-----------|-------------|----------|
    87→| **SWOT Analysis** | Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats | Brand assessment, competitive positioning, strategic planning |
    88→| **PEST / PESTEL Analysis** | Political, Economic, Social, Technological (+ Environmental, Legal) | Macro-environment scanning, market entry assessment, policy impact analysis |
    89→| **Porter's Five Forces** | Supplier bargaining power, buyer bargaining power, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, industry rivalry | Industry competitive landscape, entry barrier assessment, profit margin analysis |
    90→| **Porter's Diamond Model** | Factor conditions, demand conditions, related industries, firm strategy & structure | National/regional competitive advantage analysis |
    91→| **VRIO Analysis** | Value, Rarity, Imitability, Organization | Core competency assessment, resource advantage analysis |
    92→
    93→#### Market & Growth Analysis
    94→
    95→| Framework | Description | Best For |
    96→|-----------|-------------|----------|
    97→| **STP Analysis** | Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning | Market segmentation, target market selection, brand positioning |
    98→| **BCG Matrix (Growth-Share Matrix)** | Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, Dogs | Product portfolio management, resource allocation decisions |
    99→| **Ansoff Matrix** | Market penetration, market development, product development, diversification | Growth strategy selection |
   100→| **Product Life Cycle (PLC)** | Introduction, growth, maturity, decline | Product strategy formulation, market timing decisions |
   101→| **TAM-SAM-SOM** | Total / Serviceable / Obtainable Market | Market sizing, opportunity quantification |
   102→| **Technology Adoption Lifecycle** | Innovators → Early Adopters → Early Majority → Late Majority → Laggards | Emerging technology/category penetration analysis |
   103→
   104→#### Consumer & Behavioral Analysis
   105→
   106→| Framework | Description | Best For |
   107→|-----------|-------------|----------|
   108→| **Consumer Decision Journey** | Awareness → Consideration → Evaluation → Purchase → Loyalty | Consumer behavior path mapping, touchpoint optimization |
   109→| **AARRR Funnel (Pirate Metrics)** | Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Revenue, Referral | User growth analysis, conversion rate optimization |
   110→| **RFM Model** | Recency, Frequency, Monetary | Customer value segmentation, precision marketing |
   111→| **Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs** | Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-actualization | Consumer psychology analysis, product value proposition |
   112→| **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)** | The "job" a user needs to accomplish in a specific context | Demand insight, product innovation direction |
   113→
   114→#### Financial & Valuation Analysis
   115→
   116→| Framework | Description | Best For |
   117→|-----------|-------------|----------|
   118→| **DuPont Analysis** | ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier | Profitability decomposition, financial health diagnosis |
   119→| **DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)** | Free cash flow discounting | Enterprise/project valuation |
   120→| **Comparable Company Analysis** | PE, PB, PS, EV/EBITDA multiples comparison | Relative valuation, peer benchmarking |
   121→| **EVA (Economic Value Added)** | After-tax operating profit - Cost of capital | Value creation capability assessment |
   122→
   123→#### Competitive & Strategic Positioning
   124→
   125→| Framework | Description | Best For |
   126→|-----------|-------------|----------|
   127→| **Benchmarking** | Key performance indicator item-by-item comparison | Competitor gap analysis, best practice identification |
   128→| **Strategic Group Mapping** | Cluster competitors along two key dimensions | Competitive landscape visualization, white-space identification |
   129→| **Value Chain Analysis** | Primary activities + support activities value decomposition | Cost advantage sources, differentiation opportunity identification |
   130→| **Blue Ocean Strategy** | Value curve, four-action framework (Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create) | Differentiated innovation, new market space creation |
   131→| **Perceptual Mapping** | Plot brand positions along two consumer-perceived dimensions | Brand positioning analysis, market gap discovery |
   132→
   133→#### Industry & Supply Chain Analysis
   134→
   135→| Framework | Description | Best For |
   136→|-----------|-------------|----------|
   137→| **Industry Value Chain** | Upstream → Midstream → Downstream decomposition | Industry structure understanding, profit distribution analysis |
   138→| **Gartner Hype Cycle** | Technology Trigger → Peak of Inflated Expectations → Trough of Disillusionment → Slope of Enlightenment → Plateau of Productivity | Emerging technology maturity assessment |
   139→| **GE-McKinsey Matrix** | Industry Attractiveness × Competitive Strength | Business portfolio prioritization, investment decisions |
   140→
   141→#### Selection Principles
   142→
   143→1. **Domain-First**: Based on the domain identified in Step 1.1, select **2-4** most relevant frameworks from the toolkit above
   144→2. **Complementary**: Choose complementary rather than overlapping frameworks (e.g., macro-level with PESTEL + micro-level with Porter's Five Forces)
   145→3. **Depth over Breadth**: Better to deeply apply 2 frameworks than superficially stack 6
   146→4. **Data-Feasible**: Selected frameworks must be supportable by downstream data collection skills — if the data required by a framework cannot be reasonably obtained, downgrade or substitute
   147→5. **Explicit Mapping**: In the chapter skeleton, explicitly annotate which framework each chapter uses and how it is applied
   148→
   149→#### Framework Selection Output Format
   150→
   151→```markdown
   152→## Framework Selection
   153→
   154→| Chapter | Selected Framework(s) | Application |
   155→|---------|----------------------|-------------|
   156→| Market Size & Growth Trends | TAM-SAM-SOM + Product Life Cycle | TAM-SAM-SOM to quantify market space, PLC to determine market stage |
   157→| Competitive Landscape Assessment | Porter's Five Forces + Strategic Group Mapping | Five Forces to assess industry competition intensity, Group Mapping to visualize competitive positioning |
   158→| Consumer Profiling | RFM + Consumer Decision Journey | RFM to segment customer value, Decision Journey to identify key conversion nodes |
   159→| Brand Strategy Recommendations | SWOT + Blue Ocean Strategy | SWOT to summarize overall landscape, Blue Ocean to guide differentiation direction |
   160→```

   162→### Step 1.3: Design Chapter Skeleton
   163→
   164→Produce a hierarchical chapter structure. Each chapter must include:
   165→
   166→1. **Chapter Title** — Professional, concise, subject-based (follow titling constraints in Formatting section)
   167→2. **Analysis Objective** — What this chapter aims to reveal
   168→3. **Analysis Logic** — The reasoning chain or framework (must reference the frameworks selected in Step 1.2)
   169→4. **Core Hypothesis** — Preliminary hypotheses to be validated or refuted by data
   170→
   171→#### Chapter Skeleton Output Format
   172→
   173→```markdown
   174→## Analysis Framework
   175→
   176→### Chapter 1: [Title]
   177→- **Analysis Objective**: [This chapter aims to...]
   178→- **Analysis Logic**: [Framework or reasoning chain used]
   179→- **Core Hypothesis**: [Hypotheses to validate]
   180→- **Data Requirements**: (see Step 1.4)
   181→- **Visualization Plan**: (see Step 1.5)
   182→
   183→### Chapter 2: [Title]
   184→...
   185→```
   186→
   187→### Step 1.4: Define Data Query Requirements Per Chapter
   188→
   189→For each chapter, specify **exactly what data needs to be collected**. This is the bridge to downstream data collection skills.
   190→
   191→Each data requirement entry must include:
   192→
   193→| Field | Description |
   194→|-------|-------------|
   195→| **Data Metric** | The specific metric or data point needed (e.g., "China skincare market size 2020-2025 (in billion CNY)") |
   196→| **Data Type** | Quantitative, Qualitative, or Mixed |
   197→| **Suggested Sources** | Suggested source categories: Industry reports, financial statements, government statistics, social media, e-commerce platforms, survey data, news |
   198→| **Search Keywords** | Suggested search queries for data collection agents |
   199→| **Priority** | P0 (Required) / P1 (Important) / P2 (Supplementary) |
   200→| **Time Range** | The time period the data should cover |
   201→
   202→#### Data Requirements Output Format (per chapter)
   203→
   204→```markdown
   205→#### Data Requirements
   206→
   207→| # | Data Metric | Data Type | Suggested Sources | Search Keywords | Priority | Time Range |
   208→|---|-------------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|----------|------------|
   209→| 1 | Market size (billion CNY) | Quantitative | Industry reports, government statistics | "China skincare market size 2024" | P0 | 2020-2025 |
   210→| 2 | CAGR | Quantitative | Industry reports | "skincare CAGR growth rate" | P0 | 2020-2025 |
   211→| 3 | Sub-category share | Quantitative | E-commerce platforms, industry reports | "skincare category share cream serum sunscreen" | P1 | Latest |
   212→| 4 | Policy & regulatory updates | Qualitative | Government announcements, news | "cosmetics regulation 2024" | P2 | Past 1 year |
   213→```
   214→
   215→### Step 1.5: Define Visualization & Content Structure Per Chapter
   216→
   217→For each chapter, specify the **planned visualization** and **content structure** for the final report:
   218→
   219→| Field | Description |
   220→|-------|-------------|
   221→| **Visualization Type** | Chart type: Line chart, bar chart, pie chart, scatter plot, radar chart, heatmap, Sankey diagram, comparison table, etc. |
   222→| **Visualization Title** | Descriptive title for the chart |
   223→| **Visualization Data Mapping** | Which data indicators map to X/Y axes or segments |
   224→| **Comparison Table Design** | Column headers and comparison dimensions for the data contrast table |
   225→| **Argument Structure** | The planned "What → Why → So What" narrative outline |
   226→
   227→#### Visualization Plan Output Format (per chapter)
   228→
   229→```markdown
   230→#### Visualization & Content Plan
   231→
   232→**Chart 1**: [Type] — [Title]
   233→- X-axis: [Dimension], Y-axis: [Metric]
   234→- Data source: Corresponds to Data Requirement #1, #2
   235→
   236→**Comparison Table**:
   237→| Dimension | Item A | Item B | Item C |
   238→|-----------|--------|--------|--------|
   239→
   240→**Argument Structure**:
   241→1. **Observation (What)**: [Surface phenomenon revealed by data]
   242→2. **Attribution (Why)**: [Driving factors or underlying causes]
   243→3. **Implication (So What)**: [Strategic implications or recommended actions]
   244→```
   245→

   258→
   259→## Framework Selection
   260→
   261→| Chapter | Selected Framework(s) | Application |
   262→|---------|----------------------|-------------|
   263→| ... | ... | ... |
   264→
   265→## Chapter Skeleton
   266→
   267→### 1. [Chapter Title]
   268→- **Analysis Objective**: [...]
   269→- **Analysis Logic**: [...]
   270→- **Core Hypothesis**: [...]
   271→
   272→#### Data Requirements
   273→| # | Data Metric | Data Type | Suggested Sources | Search Keywords | Priority | Time Range |
   274→|---|-------------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|----------|------------|
   275→| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
   276→
   277→#### Visualization & Content Plan
   278→[Chart plan + Comparison table design + Argument structure]
   279→
   280→### 2. [Chapter Title]
   281→...
   282→
   283→### N. [Chapter Title]
   284→...
   285→
   286→## Data Collection Task List
   287→[Consolidate all P0/P1 data requirements across chapters into a structured task list for downstream data collection skills to execute]
   288→```

   305→
   306→After the analysis framework is generated, it is handed off to **other data collection skills** (e.g., deep-research, data-analysis, web search agents) to:
   307→
   308→1. Execute the **Search Keywords** from each chapter's data requirements

   311→4. Return a **Data Package** containing:
   312→   - **Data Summary**: Raw numbers, metrics, and qualitative findings per chapter
   313→   - **Chart Files**: Generated chart images with local file paths
   314→   - **External Search Findings**: Source URLs and summaries for citations
   315→
   316→> **This skill does NOT perform data collection.** It only produces the framework (Phase 1) and the final report (Phase 2).
   317→>
   318→> **Chart Generation**: If a visualization/charting skill is available (e.g., data-analysis, image-generation), chart generation can be deferred to the beginning of Phase 2 — see Step 2.3.

   321→
   322→# Phase 2: Report Generation
   323→
   324→## Purpose
   325→
   326→Receive the completed **Analysis Framework** and **Data Package** from upstream, and synthesize them into a final consulting-grade report.
   327→
   328→## Phase 2 Inputs
   329→
   330→| Input | Description | Required |
   331→|-------|-------------|----------|
   332→| **Analysis Framework** | The framework document produced in Phase 1 | Yes |
   333→| **Data Summary** | Collected data organized per chapter from the data collection phase | Yes |
   334→| **Chart Files** | Local file paths for generated chart images. If not provided, will be generated in Step 2.3 using available visualization skills | Optional |
   335→| **External Search Findings** | URLs and summaries for inline citations | Optional |
   336→
   337→## Phase 2 Workflow
   338→
   339→### Step 2.1: Receive and Validate Inputs
   340→
   341→Verify that all required inputs are present:
   342→
   343→1. **Analysis Framework** — Confirm it contains chapter skeleton, data requirements, and visualization plans
   344→2. **Data Summary** — Confirm it contains data organized per chapter, cross-reference against P0 requirements
   345→3. **Chart Files** — Confirm file paths are valid local paths
   346→
   347→If any P0 data is missing, note it in the report and flag for the user.
   348→
   349→### Step 2.2: Map Report Structure
   350→
   351→Map the final report structure from the Analysis Framework:
   352→
   353→1. **Abstract** — Executive summary with key takeaways
   354→2. **Introduction** — Background, objectives, methodology
   355→3. **Main Body Chapters (2...N)** — Mapped from the Framework's chapter skeleton
   356→4. **Conclusion** — Pure, objective synthesis
   357→5. **References** — GB/T 7714-2015 formatted references
   358→
   359→### Step 2.3: Generate Chapter Charts (Pre-Report Visualization)
   360→
   361→Before writing the report, generate all planned charts from the Analysis Framework's **Visualization & Content Plan**. This step ensures every sub-chapter has its "Visual Anchor" ready before narrative writing begins.
   362→
   363→#### When to Execute This Step
   364→
   365→- **Chart Files already provided**: Skip this step — proceed directly to Step 2.4.
   366→- **Chart Files NOT provided but a visualization skill is available**: Execute this step to generate all charts first.
   367→- **No Chart Files and no visualization skill available**: Skip this step — use comparison tables as the primary visual anchor in Step 2.4, and note the absence of charts.
   368→
   369→#### Chart Generation Workflow
   370→
   371→1. **Extract Chart Tasks**: Parse all `Visualization & Content Plan` entries from the Analysis Framework to build a chart generation task list:
   372→
   373→| # | Chapter | Chart Type | Chart Title | Data Mapping | Data Source |
   374→|---|---------|------------|-------------|--------------|-------------|
   375→| 1 | 2.1 | Line chart | Market Size Trend 2020-2025 | X: Year, Y: Market Size (billion CNY) | Data Requirement #1, #2 |
   376→| 2 | 3.1 | Pie chart | Consumer Age Distribution | Segments: Age groups, Values: Share % | Data Requirement #5 |
   377→| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
   378→
   379→2. **Prepare Chart Data**: For each chart task, extract the corresponding data points from the **Data Summary**.
   380→   > **CRITICAL**: Use ONLY the numbers provided in the Data Summary. Do NOT invent or "smooth" data to make charts look better. If data points are missing, the chart must reflect that reality (e.g., broken line or missing bar), or the chart type must be adjusted.
   381→
   382→3. **Delegate to Visualization Skill**: Invoke the available visualization/charting skill (e.g., `data-analysis`) for each chart task with:
   383→   - Chart type and title
   384→   - Structured data
   385→   - Axis labels and formatting preferences
   386→   - Output file path convention: `charts/chapter_{N}_{chart_index}.png`
   387→
   388→4. **Collect Chart File Paths**: Record all generated chart file paths for embedding in Step 2.4:
   389→
   390→```markdown
   391→## Generated Charts
   392→| # | Chapter | Chart Title | File Path |
   393→|---|---------|-------------|-----------|
   394→| 1 | 2.1 | Market Size Trend 2020-2025 | charts/chapter_2_1.png |
   395→| 2 | 3.1 | Consumer Age Distribution | charts/chapter_3_1.png |
   396→```
   397→
   398→5. **Validate**: Confirm all P0-priority charts have been generated. If any chart generation fails, note it and fall back to comparison tables for that sub-chapter.
   399→
   400→> **Principle**: Complete ALL chart generation before starting report writing. This ensures a consistent visual narrative and avoids interleaving generation with writing.
   401→
   402→### Step 2.4: Write the Report
   403→
   404→For each sub-chapter, follow the **"Visual Anchor → Data Contrast → Integrated Analysis"** flow:
   405→
   406→1. **Visual Evidence Block**: Embed charts using `![Image Description](Actual_File_Path)` — use the file paths collected in Step 2.3
   407→2. **Data Contrast Table**: Create a Markdown comparison table for key metrics
   408→   > **Source Rule**: Every number in the table must come from the Data Summary. No hallucinations.
   409→3. **Integrated Narrative Analysis**: Write analytical text following "What → Why → So What"
   410→   > **Narrative Rule**: Narrative must explain the *provided* data. Do not make claims unsupported by the inputs.
   411→
   412→Each sub-chapter must end with a robust analytical paragraph (min. 200 words) that:
   413→- Synthesizes conflicting or reinforcing data points
   414→- Reveals the underlying user tension or opportunity
   415→- Optionally ends with a punchy "One-Liner Truth" in a blockquote (`>`)
   416→
   417→### Step 2.5: Final Structure Self-Check
   418→
   419→Before outputting, confirm the report contains **all sections in order**:
   420→
   421→```
   422→Abstract → 1. Introduction → 2...N. Body Chapters → N+1. Conclusion → N+2. References
   423→```
   424→
   425→Additionally verify:
   426→- All charts generated in Step 2.3 are embedded in the correct sub-chapters
   427→- Chart file paths in `![](path)` references are valid
   428→- Sub-chapters without charts have comparison tables as visual anchors
   429→
   430→The report **MUST NOT** stop after the Conclusion — it **MUST** include References as the final section.
   431→
   432→## Formatting & Tone Standards
   433→
   434→### Consulting Voice
   435→- **Tone**: McKinsey/BCG — Authoritative, Objective, Professional
   436→- **Language**: All headings and content in the language specified by `output_locale`
   437→- **Number Formatting**: Use English commas for thousands separators (`1,000` not `1，000`)
   438→- **Data emphasis**: **Bold** important viewpoints and key numbers
   439→
   440→### Titling Constraints
   441→- **Numbering**: Use standard numbering (`1.`, `1.1`) directly followed by the title
   442→- **Forbidden Prefixes**: Do NOT use "Chapter", "Part", "Section" as prefixes
   443→- **Allowed Tone Words**: Analysis, Profiling, Overview, Insights, Assessment
   444→- **Forbidden Words**: "Decoding", "DNA", "Secrets", "Mindscape", "Solar System", "Unlocking"
   445→
   446→### Sub-Chapter Conclusions
   447→- **Requirement**: End each sub-chapter with a robust analytical paragraph (min. 200 words).
   448→- **Narrative Flow**: This paragraph must look like a natural continuation of the text. It must synthesize the section's findings into a strategic judgment.
   449→- **Content Logic**:
   450→    1.  Synthesize the conflicting or reinforcing data points above.
   451→    2.  Reveal the *underlying* user tension or opportunity.
   452→    3.  Key Insight: **Optional**: Only if you have a concise, punchy "One-Liner Truth", place it at the very end using a **Blockquote** (`>`) to anchor the section.
   453→
   454→### Insight Depth (The "So What" Chain)
   455→
   456→Every insight must connect **Data → User Psychology → Strategy Implication**:
   457→
   458→```
   459→❌ Bad: "Females are 60%. Strategy: Target females."
   460→
   461→✅ Good: "Females constitute 60% with a high TGI of 180. **This suggests**
   462→   the purchase decision is driven by aesthetic and social validation
   463→   rather than pure utility. **Consequently**, media spend should pivot
   464→   towards visual-heavy platforms (e.g., RED/Instagram) to maximize CTR,
   465→   treating male audiences only as a secondary gift-giving segment."
   466→```
   467→
   468→### References
   469→- **Inline**: Use markdown links for sources (e.g. `[Source Title](URL)`) when using External Search Findings
   470→- **References section**: Formatted strictly per **GB/T 7714-2015**
   471→
   472→### Markdown Rules
   473→- **Immediate Start**: Begin directly with `# Report Title` — no introductory text
   474→- **No Separators**: Do NOT use horizontal rules (`---`)
   475→
   476→## Report Structure Template
   477→
   478→```markdown
   479→# [Report Title]
   480→
   481→## Abstract
   482→[Executive summary with key takeaways]
